# Pocket guide — Berkeley Tours on the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75

A palm-sized companion to the guide: pick a walk, follow an arrow to each stop,
and hear the architect tell you about the building when you arrive.

## What's on the desk (probed 2026-10-21)

| | Found |
|---|---|
| Chip | ESP32-S3 (QFN56) rev v0.2, 240 MHz dual core, **8 MB PSRAM**, **16 MB flash** |
| USB | Native USB-Serial/JTAG at `/dev/cu.usbmodem31101` (MAC b8:1f:3f:cf:ce:88) |
| Firmware | Waveshare factory "Brookesia" image, ESP-IDF v5.5.4, LVGL, Xiaozhi AI-chat app |
| Display | 1.75" round AMOLED, 466×466, CO5300 over QSPI; CST9217 touch (responding) |
| Audio | ES8311 codec → NS4150B amp → MX1.25 speaker plug (PA enable GPIO46); ES7210 + 2 mics |
| Sensors | QMI8658 6-axis IMU (accel + gyro, **no magnetometer**); PCF85063 RTC |
| Power | AXP2101 PMIC, online; charge set to 400 mA / 4.2 V; MX1.25 battery plug |
| Storage | microSD slot (1-bit SDMMC). The boot log doesn't say whether a card is in. |
| Radio | Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz, BLE 5. **No Bluetooth Classic, so no Bluetooth headphones.** |
| GPS | **-G variant (SKU 31264), confirmed from the box**: LC76G at I2C 0x50/0x54, UART on GPIO17/18, reset on TCA9554 P7 |
| In the box | Enclosed speaker (already plugged into SPK) and a ceramic GPS patch antenna (IPEX lead, **not yet clipped onto the "GPS" socket**). Nothing in BAT. |

Pin map and I2C addresses are in Waveshare's `HARDWARE_REFERENCE.md`
(github.com/waveshareteam/ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75). The expansion header
gives VBUS, GND, 3V3, GPIO43/44 (UART0), GPIO17, GPIO18 and GPIO16.

## The three hard constraints

1. **Location comes from the onboard LC76G.** It needs the ceramic antenna
   clipped on, facing the sky. Plan on a 30–60 s cold start outdoors, and
   expect it not to fix indoors at the desk.
2. **There is no magnetometer, so there's no true compass out of the box.** Plan:
   - **v1:** fuse the gyro with GPS course-over-ground. While you walk, GPS gives
     the true heading. When you stop, the gyro holds and tracks turns. It drifts
     only after a long, still pause. That's enough for "the next stop is ahead
     and to the left".
   - **Optional:** add a magnetometer breakout (LIS3MDL, MMC5603 or QMC5883P) on
     a second I2C bus using GPIO43/44 (move the console to USB-JTAG). That gives
     a real compass while standing still. It needs a figure-8 calibration and has
     to sit away from the speaker magnet.
3. **Audio comes out of the speaker only.** The S3 can't do A2DP, so
   narration plays through the small speaker. You'll need an 8 Ω, ~1 W speaker
   with an MX1.25 plug.

## Shopping list

- 3.7 V LiPo with MX1.25 2-pin plug, 800–1000 mAh. Check the plug's polarity against the BAT silkscreen before connecting.
- microSD card, 8–32 GB, FAT32
- Optional: a magnetometer breakout

The speaker and GPS antenna came with the board.

## Architecture

```
device/                       ESP-IDF project (C), lives in this repo
  main/
    main.c     display up, screens built, console started
    console.c  USB console: snap, show, walk, tap, swipe, stats, where
    ui/        LVGL 9 screens, motion engine, companion portrait, scenes
    fonts/     Jost + Berkeley Post (generated by build-fonts.sh)
    art/       portraits, scenes, arrow, cover (generated by build-art.mjs)
    gnss/      (to come) LC76G (I2C) or NMEA-over-UART reader → fix {lat, lon, acc, cog, speed}
    heading/   (to come) gyro + COG complementary filter (+ magnetometer if fitted)
    guide/     (to come) tours, stops, arrival logic ported from lib/arrival.ts
    audio/     (to come) MP3 from SD → esp_audio_codec → ES8311; chime; volume
    power/     (to come) AXP2101 gauge, screen dim/off, GNSS duty cycle
  backup/      factory flash image (gitignored)
scripts/device/
  build-fonts.sh  Jost / Berkeley Post → LVGL fonts
  build-art.mjs   the app's SVG art → layered RGB565 pictures
  snap.py         drive the board over USB and save its screen as PNG
  export.ts       (to come) guide data → SD bundle (one source of truth: data/ + the guide editor)
  bake-tiles      (to come) dark AMOLED map tiles from assets/map/berkeley.pmtiles
  narrate         (to come) scripts → TTS → loudness-normalised MP3
```

There's no SDL simulator after all: the board itself is the simulator.
`snap.py` captures its screen and feeds it simulated touches over USB, which
shows the real fonts, colours and frame rate, where a desktop build wouldn't.

Stack: ESP-IDF v6.0.2 (Waveshare tests their examples on it; the Mac's Python
3.14 rules out v5.5), Waveshare BSP component `waveshare/esp32_s3_touch_amoled_1_75`
(LVGL through `esp_lvgl_adapter`), LVGL 9.4, `espressif/esp_audio_codec` for
MP3, `minmea` for NMEA.

### SD card bundle

```
/guide/guide.json         tours (12), stops (77 unique), routes, per-stop text + audio refs
/guide/tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}.jpg   z13–z17 over the core, dark ground (AMOLED black = off pixels = battery)
/guide/audio/{landmarkId}.mp3  ~60–90 s each, 64 kbps mono (~0.5 MB) — ~45 MB for all tour stops
/guide/art/{architect}.png     cut-paper portraits for the arrival card
```

The existing `assets/tiles.bin` (JPEG, z11–z16, 6.4 MB) shows the pipeline
works. It's baked from OSM raster tiles in the light paper style, though. For
the device, re-bake from the vector `berkeley.pmtiles` in a navy/black style
with a z17 layer, which also avoids bulk-pulling OSM tiles again.

### Screens (round 466×466)

1. **Walks.** Rotary list of the 12 walks ("Walk 01 · Downtown Berkeley"),
   showing distance and time.
2. **Follow.** A pointer on the rim toward the next stop, metres remaining, stop
   name, and a progress ring around the bezel (stops done of total).
3. **Arrived.** Fires on arrival (40 m, fix ≤ 50 m, per `lib/arrival.ts`), with a
   chime. Shows the architect's cut-paper portrait, name, architect and year,
   then the narration plays automatically with a ring scrubber. Tap to pause,
   swipe for the next stop.
4. **Map.** A tile view centred on you, with the route line and stop dots.
   Pinch or double-tap to zoom.
5. **Settings.** Volume, brightness, auto-play, GPS status, battery.

Style follows design.md: Jost, Berkeley Post for titles, cream on near-black
navy (#0B2E8C accents) with vermilion, and flat planes rather than faux-antique
texture. Copy calls it "the guide" and walks are "Walk 01".

### Screen design (2026-10-21)

Mockups and a clickable flow: the "Pocket Guide Screens" design canvas
(claude.ai/artifact/SXB4RGdZPPGfKVCepJiJzY), nine round 466 px screens in three
rows: Setting out (Cover, Choose a walk, Finding you), On the walk (Follow,
Map, Controls), At each stop (At the door, Listening, Walked).

- **The screen is a travel label.** The round display is one of the guide's
  circle stamps, so the same press sheet applies: paper `#FAF6EC` for the
  guide's pages, the blue board `#0A2C8D` for the cover, the controls shade and
  the walked stamp. Follow has a Paper/Board switch to try outdoors; the board
  is the low-power option on AMOLED.
- **Inks keep their jobs from the app.** Blue for buttons, kickers and
  collected stops; vermilion only for the route arrow, the reader's mark, the
  stop in progress and stamps; blue tint for what's ahead.
- **Type.** Jost throughout (light for big numerals and titles, medium tracked
  capitals for kickers), Berkeley Post only on the cover.
- **The bezel is the walk.** The app's stop ticks bend into a ring: one
  segment per stop. While listening, the same ring shows narration progress.
- **Art is the app's own.** Walk labels, architect portraits, the Campanile
  device and the walk plates, baked to the panel rather than redrawn.
- **Two buttons, and touch.** The side buttons carry the walk (see "Getting
  about" below); a tap does the one thing on screen, a swipe turns the walks,
  and the handle on Follow pulls the controls down. The guide moves itself
  on: it finds you, points the way, stamps the label at the door, plays the
  stop, and points you on.
- **Copy from the app.** "Set out on foot", "Stop 5 of 10", "On to stop 6",
  "min walk", "at the door", "All 10 labels collected". Captions show the
  sentence being spoken, for noisy streets.
- **Sizes.** Body 16–19 px, kickers 14–15 px tracked, titles 27–32 px, the
  distance 58–66 px; touch targets at least 48 px, main actions 56–84 px; text
  kept inside the circle's safe chord.

### Legibility pass (2026-10-22)

The panel is about 266 px per inch, so 16 px type is only 4.3 pt. A review
of the board's screens raised it: the caption is 20 px (unspoken words
darker), body and speech slips 18 px, kickers and button tabs 16 px (new
fonts `jost_regular_20/22`, `jost_medium_18`). The buttons' tabs are the only
Back/Next/Done: the on-screen Back (Map), Next stop and Done word (Controls)
went. Controls moved down so the NEXT STOP tab clears the Volume row, the
stamps moved off the host's face, and the narration screens gained a "15"
button that goes back fifteen seconds. Stamp lettering is 16 px and the credit italic 18. The lower button now only ever goes back or opens the menu (on Walks it returns to the cover; turning walks is swipe or the chevrons), and the Controls menu has a Map button beside Last stop.

The map (schematic, until the Phase 6 tiles): every stop is a 22 px disc with
its number, and touching one names it on the slip at the top; the leg from you
to the next stop is drawn in vermilion; a scale bar gives the route's scale.
It still has no streets: that needs the baked tiles and the SD card.

### Getting about (2026-10-22)

Walking a walk the first time, it wasn't clear how to get from one stop to
the next, or out of the walk. The two side buttons now carry it, and each
screen says what they do on two small tabs inside the rim beside them
(tapping a tab is the same as pressing its button):

- **Upper (PWR, about two o'clock): go on.** Cover → walks → set out →
  start; on a walk, *I'm here* on Follow, *Listen* at the door, *Next stop*
  (*Finish* at the last) while a stop plays and on the controls. A short
  press only: held a few seconds, the power manager switches the board off.
- **Lower (BOOT, about four o'clock): the menu.** On a walk it brings the
  controls down over whatever's showing, and puts them back up (*Done*). Off
  a walk it goes back (Finding → Walks, Map → Follow); on the walks it turns
  to the next walk.
- **Lower, held a second: end the walk.** It asks first, on the board ("End
  the walk?", the labels so far): upper *End*, lower *Keep*. Ending shows the
  walk's stamp with what was collected ("3 of 10"; *Walked* when it's all).
  End walk on the controls asks the same.

On their own, the stops go round: near the door (25 m) the guide stamps the
label; seven seconds later the stop's narration starts; when it's told, the
next stop's way comes up. A stop is narrated by its architect when they're
drawn (a handover first if it isn't the host's own), otherwise by the guide
with the host listening, credited to whoever's voice it is.

The walks are real now: `scripts/device/export-walks.ts` writes
`device/main/ui/walks_gen.c` from the app's data (each stop's name, who
narrates it, its opening lines, and its distance and bearing from the stop
before). Until the GNSS drives Follow (Phase 4) the distance is simulated at
walking pace from those, and the Map draws each walk's route from them,
north up, in place of Downtown's plate. Walks whose host isn't drawn yet
(Howard's and Hays's) or that have none (Industrial Heritage) show their
label scene.

Console, for testing at the desk: `button upper|lower|hold` presses one,
`buttons` shows each button's state and the last eight real presses, `stop n`
jumps to a stop, `where` says the screen, walk, stop, labels and distance.

### Motion and the companion (2026-10-21, second pass)

Everything moves, the way the app's living illustrations do: paper-toy
motion, where pieces slide, squash and swap, eased, with no springs. Reduce
Motion stills it.

- **Walk scenes fill the screen.** Each walk's label scene is drawn
  full-bleed in the circle (its clip and outline dropped, framed on a square of
  the scene), with the title on a paper slip. The sun or moon (`data-anim="sky"`)
  crosses the sky, clouds (`data-anim="cloud"`) drift, Piedmont's sun sinks
  into its vanishing point, the scene breathes in a slow zoom, and a new walk
  slides in on a swipe.
- **One host per walk (decided 2026-10-21).** The walk's lead architect, or
  failing that its most-featured one: `architectsOnTour(tour)` in
  `lib/architects.ts`, `lead ?? cast[0]`, ties going to whoever appears first.
  The host walks with you the whole way. At a stop another architect narrates,
  the host steps aside and hands over ("Over to James Plachek."), then comes
  back when the narration ends. At a stop the guide narrates, the host stays
  on screen and listens. The export writes `host` on each walk in `guide.json`.

  | Walk | Host | Walk | Host |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 01 Downtown | Ratcliff | 07 Historic Landmarks | Plachek |
  | 02 South of Campus | Maybeck | 08 Maybeck Trail | Maybeck |
  | 03 Northside & Hills | Maybeck | 09 Julia Morgan Legacy | Morgan |
  | 04 UC Berkeley Campus | Howard | 10 Buena Vista & Hilltop | Maybeck |
  | 05 West Berkeley Heritage | Ratcliff | 11 Industrial Heritage | none |
  | 06 Elmwood & Claremont | Morgan | 12 Piedmont Avenue Stroll | Hays |

  Walk 11 has no architect with a portrait, so it has no host: the guide
  speaks throughout, and the walk's label scene stands where the companion
  would. Walk 05's host, Ratcliff, narrates only its last stop.
- **The companion, Tamagotchi-style.** The host fills the screen (third
  pass, 2026-10-21: "the person should cover the screen, not be confined to a
  small box"). No frame: the portrait is drawn at 2.8 px a unit, the face a
  little above the middle, the shoulders running off the foot of the circle,
  on the portrait's own ground colour (Ratcliff and Yelland peach, Plachek
  and Howard blue, Hays and Coxhead charcoal, Maybeck and Thomas vermilion,
  Morgan and Gutterson blue tint, Esherick paper). Faces drawn smaller than the rest are brought up to
  size (Morgan, ×1.25). On the dark grounds the marks go cream; on vermilion
  the accent goes cream. Everything else sits on top: a slip held up in front
  for what matters (the distance, the caption, the stop), speech slips beside
  the head, buttons at the sides, the rim round the edge. It changes mood
  with the walk, each mood built from the portrait's marked parts
  (`data-anim="eye" | "brow" | "mouth"`, the `data-smile` shape):
  - *Walking* (Follow): bobs in step, blinks, grins now and then, and looks
    toward the pointer on the rim.
  - *Searching* (Finding you): eyes dart, head peers side to side, while a
    sweep runs round the rim.
  - *Glad* (At the door, Walked): smile held, double blink, a small hop; the
    label stamp presses onto the corner of the slip.
  - *Talking* (Listening): mouth moves in an irregular syllable rhythm, head
    nods; paused, the architect idles and waits.
  - *Handing over* (another architect's stop): the stop's architect wipes in
    on their own ground and fills the screen, brows up, smiling; the host
    shrinks into a small round cameo at the left, looking across.
  - *Listening* (a stop the guide narrates): the host fills the screen above
    the caption slip, eyes on it, nodding now and then.
  - *Dozing* (Resting): eyes closed, slow nod, breathing, rising z's.
  - *Speaking slips*: short lines in a paper slip ("That's my building ahead.",
    "Our first stop: City Hall.", "Well walked."), taken from the guide's facts.
- **Follow points with the rim, not a big arrow.** With the host filling the
  screen, the way to the next stop is a vermilion arrowhead riding just
  inside the rim at the bearing, with its cut-paper shadow; the host's eyes
  follow it.
- **Resting** is the between-stops, screen-dimmed state on the blue board: the
  architect asleep and dimmed, the next stop and distance on a night slip,
  "Tap to wake".
- **Everything else**: the pointer sways toward its heading and the distance
  counts down; the current stop's ring segment pulses; the stamp presses in
  with an ink ring; the narration ring advances and the caption lights word by
  word; on the cover the sun rises behind the Campanile, clouds drift, the
  lettering ring turns and the title prints in; on the map the reader's mark
  pulses, the heading wedge sways and the stop flag flutters; the controls
  shade drops in row by row.
- **On the device**: these become LVGL animations on pre-split portrait layers
  (body, head, eyes, mouth, smile) and scene layers (sky, clouds, ground), so
  only the parts that move redraw. A full-screen portrait is about 445 KB of
  art (Ratcliff and Plachek are baked so far); all eleven would be about
  4.9 MB, so before baking the whole cast, compress them (LVGL's RLE) or move
  them to the SD card.

## Narration ("in the architect's voice")

- **Scope v1:** the 77 unique stops on the 12 walks. All 406 entries come later.
  All 12 walks are drafted in `data/narration/`: 77 stop scripts, 12 intros and
  outros, about 67 minutes of unique audio. The `checks:` in each file list
  where the guide's entry disagrees with its sources.
- **Source of truth is the app.** Scripts draw only on the guide's entry
  (`data/landmarks.json`), the architects in `lib/architects.ts`, and the BAHA
  and Daily Planet articles in `data/scraped/`. Anything the narration needs
  that the guide lacks goes into the entry first, through the guide editor.
- **Cast:** the eleven notable architects in `lib/architects.ts`, the ones with
  portraits, narrate their own buildings in the first person. Every other
  stop is read by the guide's narrator. An architect never speaks about events
  after their lifetime. Later history goes in a short narrator coda.
  Each walk's intro says the architects' voices are dramatizations.
- **Scripts:** 100–200 words (40–80 s). Each one is walk-agnostic, because a
  landmark can sit on several walks: no "next stop" lines. Walk intros and
  outros live in their own file.
- **Voices (cast 2026-10-22):** original TTS characterizations, not clones,
  each made from a written brief and matched to the portrait: the age in the
  face, the build of the frame, the look in the eyes. Maybeck's white beard
  and shut, smiling eyes make a slow, airy baritone of about 80 (125 words a
  minute); Howard's moustache under the tallest frame, a resonant
  bass-baritone; Ratcliff's round glasses and grin, a brisk light tenor;
  Morgan, a quiet, exact mezzo. The guide, undrawn and two thirds of the
  listening (about 46 of 78 minutes), is cast against the rest: a warm
  contemporary alto, so she's never mistaken for an architect. Every brief,
  with register, pace, speech, grain and manner, is on the design canvas
  ("Voices" row), along with a chart that keeps the eleven apart by pitch and
  pace. Gutterson and Esherick are cast but have no lines on the 12 walks yet.
- **Render:** Qwen3-TTS (Apache-2.0), run locally on the Mac at about real
  time, free, with nothing sent anywhere. The cast lives in
  `data/narration/voices.json` (each voice's brief, target pitch and pace, and
  an audition line from its own script). `scripts/narration/design_takes.py`
  makes takes from the briefs and `measure.py` checks their pitch and pace;
  takes land in `narration-takes/` (not in git). Briefs state pitch and pace
  outright: left to words like "alto", the model ran higher and quicker. A
  picked take becomes the voice's reference, and every line after is cloned
  from it (Qwen's Base model), so a voice doesn't drift between stops. Then
  loudness to −16 LUFS → 64 kbps mono MP3. A voice change is just a re-render.
- **Locked (2026-10-22):** all twelve (`lock_voices.py`; references in
  `data/narration/voices/*.flac`). `speak.py` speaks lines in a locked voice;
  `check_voices.py` and `apart.py` compare voices by speaker fingerprint
  (`fingerprint.py`), each against the voices of its own register, with four
  calibration voices from outside the cast filling out the high register.
  (Centred on the whole cast, ten men to two women, any two women scored about
  0.7 alike, which first made the guide and Julia Morgan look too alike.) All
  ten voices with scripts hold on a line they had never spoken: 0.62–0.77 to
  their own reference, 0.10–0.34 to the most alike other. Julia Morgan was
  recast anyway, in her own era (about 55, reedy, crisp period diction), and
  sits further from the guide (0.10). Most takes read 10–30% quicker than
  their briefs; pace is set at render time.
- **How a stop plays:** the door holds 7 s, a guest's voice starts 0.4 s after
  their screen, a change of speaker within a script (the guide's coda) is a
  0.7 s gap with the credit changing and no sound, and 1.5 s after the last
  word the way to the next stop comes up. The walk's introduction plays on
  Finding you, the outro on Walked. The companion's speech slips stay silent.
  The canvas ("Sound" row) times Walk 01's library stop end to end.

### Sound (2026-10-22)

Ten short sounds, struck rather than played, synthesized on the device the
way the chime already is (`audio/sound.c`): bells in C major pentatonic for
the walk's moments (Found you, At the door, On you go, Walked, Lost the sky),
a felt thump for the stamp, wooden ticks for the buttons, and a paper slide
for the controls shade and the handover. Each architect's handover bell is a
note of the scale, lower for deeper voices (Howard C5 … Morgan C7). All of
it sits between 500 Hz and 6 kHz for the small speaker, 6 dB under the
voices (ticks 12 dB under), with 4 ms attacks; nothing loops, nothing plays
between stops but the ticks and Lost the sky, and volume 0 is silent. The
canvas plays every one from the same recipe the firmware will use.

### Script format

```
data/narration/
  walks/<tourId>.md     ## intro / ## outro, read by the guide
  stops/<landmarkId>.md one per landmark, shared by every walk it's on
```

Frontmatter: `landmark` (or `walk`), `title`, `status` (draft | reviewed),
`sources` (repo paths), and `checks` (where the guide's entry disagrees with
its sources, to fix in the editor). The body is split by `## <speaker>`
headings, where a speaker is an `ArchitectKey` or `guide`. Sections play in
order.

### Phase 0 results (2026-10-21)

- **Factory backup:** `device/backup/factory-b81f3fcfce88.bin`, all 16 MB
  (SHA-256 `ed11e9e668ca0ab92c7aa6e1dab5dd913bae3387ee8f1aa0712c9a74ca2ec90f`).
  Restore with `esptool --chip esp32s3 -p /dev/cu.usbmodem31101 write-flash 0 <file>`.
- **Toolchain:** ESP-IDF v6.0.2 in `~/esp/esp-idf` (`source ~/esp/esp-idf/export.sh`).
  Build and flash from `device/`: `idf.py build`, then `idf.py -p /dev/cu.usbmodem31101 flash`.
  The app is 8.0 MB, almost all art, in a 12 MB partition.
- **On the board:** all twelve screens from the canvas: Cover, Walks (all 12
  scenes), Finding, Follow, Map, Controls, At the door, Listening, The guide
  narrates, Handing over, Resting and Walked, with the companion's moods, the
  moving skies, stamps, captions and slips. Hosts baked so far: Ratcliff,
  Plachek, Maybeck, Morgan (Howard and Hays to come). Checked by capture and
  by simulated touch through the whole flow: BEGIN, flick and slow-drag
  between walks, both side buttons, set out, find, follow, arrive, listen,
  pause, skip, the controls shade down and up, the map and back, end walk,
  Walked, Resting and Handing over.
- **Still simulated:** Finding is real for every walk (its host from the
  table above, its first stop and size from the guide; a walk without a baked
  host shows its scene, and the guide says nothing). Everything after it is
  Walk 01: stop 5 (Chamber of Commerce) on Follow, the map and the door,
  Ratcliff's narration, the guide's stop 6, Plachek's handover at stop 8; the
  distance counts down on its own, tapping the distance slip stands in for
  arriving, and the narration clock runs without sound. The data export
  (Phase 2), GNSS (Phase 4) and audio (Phase 5) replace these.
- **Frame rates** (`snap.py stats`): Cover 58 fps, Walks redraws only when
  its sky moves (94 % idle). With the full-screen companion, Finding runs at
  27 fps and Follow at 30–33, with LVGL nearly always busy: most of each frame
  is the host's head bob, which redraws a 240 × 270 px patch. Smooth enough;
  for battery, see Phase 8 (fewer bob steps, a lower frame cap while walking).
- **What a frame costs** (`snap.py bench`, a whole-screen redraw): 29 ms just
  to fill the screen and send it to the panel, the ceiling being about
  35 fps for anything that redraws everything; a full-screen portrait adds
  15 ms. So nothing that animates may cover the whole screen: Finding's
  rings spreading from behind the head cost 25 ms more and held it to 13 fps,
  and were replaced by a sweep round the rim. Fading a large object with
  `opa` paints it off-screen first; fade a border or arc colour instead.
- **Still simulated:** Finding and Follow show Walk 01's host and lines (Ratcliff,
  "Our first stop: City Hall.", stop 5, 180 m counting down) whichever walk is
  chosen, and tapping the mark stands in for a GPS fix. The data export
  (Phase 2) and the GNSS (Phase 4) replace these.
- **Where the board differs from the canvas:** Handing over slides the
  guest's ground up whole rather than wiping it in a widening circle; the
  stamps carry only "WALK 01" and the date round the edge, as the walk's name
  doesn't fit at the guide's smallest type. The cover's ring of lettering
  holds still. Turning it redraws the whole screen at every step, and the S3
  manages that only about ten times a second, slowing everything else on the
  cover. The scene's slow 26 s breathe-zoom is left out for the same reason.

What it took, for next time:

- **Keep art out of PSRAM.** Waveshare's example config copies code and
  read-only data into PSRAM (`SPIRAM_XIP_FROM_PSRAM`, `SPIRAM_RODATA`). With
  7 MB of embedded art that left LVGL 960 KB, so both are off: art is read
  from flash.
- **No `LV_ATTRIBUTE_FAST_MEM_USE_IRAM`.** With IDF 6's GCC 15 it fails to
  build (conflicting `.iram1.N` sections on declarations).
- **Style setters always redraw,** even with an unchanged value, so every
  motion step checks first (`ui/motion.c`). That took Follow from 19 fps to 47.
  Image setters already check.
- **Box shadows need `LV_DRAW_SW_SHADOW_CACHE_SIZE`,** or a fading slip
  re-blurs its shadow every frame.
- **Float on (`LV_USE_FLOAT`)** for letters set on an arc: in whole degrees
  their spacing goes ragged ("BER KEL EY").
- **The USB console drops output** the Mac doesn't collect within 50 ms, so
  captures write straight to the driver, blocking. Opening the port sets
  DTR then RTS, and RTS-high-with-DTR-low resets the chip; `snap.py` drops
  RTS first, so connecting doesn't reboot the board.
- **Slow swipes are clicks** to LVGL, which only reports quick ones as
  gestures. The walk picker measures how far the finger went before setting out.

### Phase 1 results (2026-10-21)

Built into the guide's own firmware rather than a separate test image:
drivers in `device/main/{power,heading,gnss,storage,audio}/`, polled by
`hw.c`; console checks (`i2c`, `pmu`, `imu`, `gps`, `sd`, `chime`, plus
`reg`, `i2cw`/`i2cr`, `uart` for digging); and a **Hardware check** screen
for testing away from the Mac: on Follow, pull down the controls and tap the
"Following you" line.

| Part | Result |
|---|---|
| I2C bus | Answers: codec 0x18, expander 0x20, power 0x34, mics 0x40, GNSS 0x50, clock 0x51, touch 0x5A, motion 0x6B |
| Motion (QMI8658) | Working: 1.01 g down lying flat, gyro steady within ±1.5 °/s |
| Power (AXP2101) | Working: USB 5.05 V, system 3.73 V, no battery yet. All its regulators were already on; nothing changed |
| Speaker | The codec opens and plays the arrival chime (two bell notes, E6 then C6, synthesised) and test tones. Whether it's loud enough on a street is still to hear |
| SD card | Slot answers "no card"; needs a FAT32 microSD |
| GPS (LC76G) | Answers on I2C, but its transmitter had stopped: see below |

**The GPS jam.** On this module (LC76GABMD) the I2C transmitter stops if its
buffer goes unread for long, and then only a long power-off restarts it; reset
pulses and PMIC tricks don't (Quectel forum, same module on an ESP32-S3). The
board ran for hours with nothing reading it, and our first reader made it
worse: the S3 abandons a held clock after 2 ms, the module holds it for up to
~200 ms, and every abandoned answer leaves it stuck. `gnss.c` now gives it
300 ms, a separate frame for each step with 10 ms between, the bus to itself
for the whole exchange (`board_bus_lock`), drains its 4 KB buffer every
200 ms in 512-byte pieces, and clears a stuck answer by reading a byte at
0x54 and 0x58. The UART pins (GPIO 17/18) are behind unfitted resistors;
nothing comes out.

Two more power-offs found the real cause. **At its default output (every
sentence, four constellations) the module fills its 4 KB I2C buffer about four
seconds after power-up, and then its transmitter sleeps**: it refuses the
length request and sends nothing, and no write, reset pulse or recovery read
wakes it; its backup domain (V_BCKP, from the PMIC's RTC regulator) carries
the state through everything but a power-off. In manual mode, reading by hand
after the second power-off found the buffer still holding everything from
power-up (the module's banner: firmware LC76GABNR12A05S, 2025-09-25), which
drained cleanly (8,690 bytes, no bad checksums), and then nothing more: the
output had stopped at 00:00:03 after power-up. (My `gps probe` had also been
misleading: reading the answer before the module is ready makes it drop the
request. The reader's order, 10 ms after each step, is right.)

So the GNSS reader now starts before anything else (`main.c`: bus, lock and
reader at ~1.8 s, asking every 200 ms while it boots), and on its first good
exchange cuts the output to what the guide uses: GLL, GSA and
VTG off, GSV every tenth fix, GGA and RMC each second (`$PAIR062`), about
200 B/s, so the buffer takes ~20 s to fill. The first time ever it also saves
that in the module (`$PAIR513`, re-sent each boot until the module answers
`$PAIR001,513,0`; reported not to stick on some sister modules, so check after
a power-off). Only then does the display come up, with the bus lock held, so
the touch set-up can't fall between a GNSS request and its answer; touch
reads afterwards wait for the lock too (`hw.c` wraps LVGL's touch read).

The fourth power-off showed the rest. The module saved the quieter output
(it acknowledged `$PAIR001,513,0`), but **any command written to it leaves it
stuck**: it answers "nothing waiting" or refuses until it has settled (tens of
seconds), after which a byte read at 0x54 and 0x58 and two quick exchanges
bring it back. (Restarts mid-exchange can do the same.) So in normal running
the reader sends nothing; if it hears nothing for three seconds it tries
that recovery every eight until the module speaks; and it only sends the
output settings if they've changed (a version kept in flash) or it hears the
noisy sentences. A flash keeps the ESP32 from reading for about 40 s, which
at the first quiet setting (~170 B/s) filled the ~9 KB buffer and slept the
module again; version 2 of the setting (RMC each second, GGA every other,
GSV every thirtieth, ~110 B/s) holds about 80 s. The lasting fix is a short
flash: move the 8 MB of art out of the app image into the SD bundle (Phase 2)
so `app-flash` takes seconds.

(The third power-off was lost to a start-up hang of my making: the touch
wrapper went in before the bus lock existed, start-up stalled, the reader
never ran, and the module filled up and slept again. `board_init()` now
creates the lock first, and a wait of over two seconds for the bus logs who
holds it.) Commands go to the module through
`gnss_send()`: room query (0xAA510004), length (0xAA531000), then the
sentence to 0x58.

To finish Phase 1:

1. **GPS:** unplug the guide (USB, and battery if fitted) for ten minutes and
   plug it back in; it reads and quietens the module by itself. Check `gps`:
   bytes should keep climbing, about 200 a second. Then outdoors (on a USB power bank, or with the laptop),
   open the Hardware check and wait for "Fix" (a cold start can take a few
   minutes). The first fix is kept in flash; `gps log` reads it back.
2. **Speaker:** plug it into the MX1.25 socket, tap "Play the chime".
3. **SD card and battery:** fit them when they arrive; the screen reports both.

## Risks

- **GPS under trees and near buildings** (Northside, campus): LC76G is
  multi-constellation, but expect 10–20 m error. Keep the 50 m accuracy gate,
  and add a manual "I'm here" tap.
- **Heading at walking pace:** COG is noisy below ~1 m/s. Smooth it and fall
  back to the gyro.
- **Speaker loudness on the street.** Test early in phase 1.
- **Round-screen layout:** keep text inside the centre ~330 px square.
- **I can't see the physical screen.** `scripts/device/snap.py` captures the
  board's screen over USB and drives it with simulated touches, so layout,
  colour and frame rate are checked on the real panel. How it feels in the
  hand, AMOLED brightness outdoors, and the real walks need you.
