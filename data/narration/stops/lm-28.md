## guide

The United States Post Office, finished in 1914. It came from the office of Oscar Wenderoth, Supervising Architect of the Treasury, the bureau in Washington that drew the country's federal buildings.

Berkeley campaigned for it. The Chamber of Commerce counted a proper federal post office among its wins of 1914, the same year it persuaded the Southern Pacific to make Berkeley a regular stop on its main line.

Its classical front was meant to say that the national government had arrived in Berkeley and meant to stay. It belongs to the Civic Center Historic District, alongside City Hall and the Veterans Memorial Building.
