## maybeck

Bernard Maybeck. In 1899 I was teaching drawing at the university, and the Town and Gown Club asked me for a clubhouse. The club was the work of Madame Paget, a formidable Frenchwoman who had organized the women of Berkeley the year before. They had bought this corner, and they had 2,500 dollars to build on it.

So: a plain box of redwood shingles, with the roof carried out on long brackets you can see from the street. Inside, redwood everywhere and no plaster at all. A low ground floor, eight feet high, for the library and the kitchen. Above it, a meeting hall thirty feet high, with the underside of the roof painted a bluish green. Even the furniture was made to order, and kept plain.

The San Francisco Call came to look, and reported curiosity about its "peculiar oddity and eccentricity of design." I've had worse reviews.

## guide

In 1902 the club joined the Hillside Club in getting Berkeley to plant shade trees along its main streets, and the town made the first Monday of December its Arbor Day.
