## guide

The Church of the Good Shepherd, 1878. It began with a women's sewing society, which started collecting money in 1877 to build an Episcopal church in West Berkeley. The building went up the next year, the same year Berkeley became a town. It is the oldest church building standing in Berkeley, and the oldest in the East Bay still used by the congregation that built it.

The architect, Charles Bugbee, modeled it on a Presbyterian church in Mendocino designed by his father's San Francisco firm. Then he loosened it up: Victorian ornament on the walls, patterned shingles on the spire. Look at the buttresses holding up the tower. They're hollow wooden boxes.

The eighty-foot tower holds a thousand-pound bell. Until 1894 it served as the neighborhood fire alarm too.

In October 2012 an electrical fire gutted the sanctuary. The church was restored, and reopened in September 2014.
