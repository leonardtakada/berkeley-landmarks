## guide

The J. J. Pfister Knitting Mill. John Jacob Pfister, a Swiss immigrant, started knitting in San Francisco in 1877 with three hand-operated machines, and built the business into one of the city's leading makers of bathing suits, sweaters and athletic uniforms.

The 1906 earthquake and fire changed his plans, and that summer he chose West Berkeley for a new factory. The architect was William Wharff, who the year before had designed the Masonic Temple downtown. It opened in November 1906: two stories, fifteen windows down each side, yarn stored in the basement, the knitting machines on the first floor and finishing on the second. The Pfisters lived across the street, in a shingled cottage Wharff also designed.

The company couldn't survive the slump after the First World War. Pfister died in 1921, and in 1929 a seed company took over the building and covered its shingles in stucco.
