## yelland

William Yelland. I grew up in Saratoga, studied architecture at the University here, and then the war sent me to France. I came home with Normandy in my head, the farmhouses and steep roofs and old brick, and I never quite got it out.

In 1925 John Tupper and Lawrence Reed, who ran the music store, asked me for a new building on Shattuck. A music shop ought to look like it came out of a story, so I gave them one: brick walls, a roof pitched as steep as a hat, and a piper on the chimney, high-stepping, as if he were leading the customers in.

It got noticed. The next year a young builder named Jack Thornburg had me design the first part of a whole village of these on Spruce Street. People call it Normandy Village.

Look up at the chimney for the piper.

## guide

The building has since been the Sign of the Piper restaurant and a run of cafés and pubs. Today it's a cocktail bar that took the old store's name.
