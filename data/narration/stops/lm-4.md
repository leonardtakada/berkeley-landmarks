## morgan

Julia Morgan. This was St. John's Presbyterian Church. Its congregation formed in 1907, some of them families who had lost their homes in San Francisco the year before, and they wanted a church for the lowest possible price.

So Walter Steilberg and I built it of plain studs and planks of Douglas fir, and left the whole structure showing inside: the posts, the beams, the trusses, even the hardware. The pattern of the timbers is the ornament. The light comes in through clerestory windows of smoked glass, the kind used in factories, and the lamps are wood and iron, with bare bulbs pointing down. Outside, there are broad gables, stained shingles and clapboard.

It cost a dollar sixty a square foot. The sanctuary was finished in 1910. It was job number 237 in my office.

## guide

In 1974 the congregation moved to a new church down the street, and this building faced demolition. The fight to save it helped bring the Berkeley Architectural Heritage Association into being. Its timber and its fine acoustics have made it a performing arts center ever since, named for its architect.
