## coxhead

Ernest Coxhead. I trained in England as an architect of churches. In 1886 my brother Almeric and I set up practice in Los Angeles, and four years later we moved to San Francisco.

In 1903 Allen Freeman, the general manager of a big fruit company, asked us for a house here, on the north fork of Strawberry Creek. His neighbor across the creek had built Weltevreden in clinker brick, the dark, lumpy brick the kilns used to throw away. We used it too. Above it I put a steep roof with oversized gambrel dormers to the north and south, and in front a porch with arched openings and a classical balustrade. Rustic outside, formal and rich within.

The Freemans opened the house in the fall of 1904 with cello and piano recitals. Mrs. Freeman's invitations said "Evening Dress," which was unheard of in Berkeley, and the town's dressmakers had to hurry.

## guide

The house was later home to Robert Sibley, who founded the East Bay Regional Park District in 1934.
