## guide

The Masonic Temple, at Bancroft and Shattuck, completed in April 1907.

Its architect, William Wharff, had a life bigger than the building. Born in Maine in 1836, he learned the carpenter's trade from his uncle and never went to architecture school. He fought for the Union, saw Lincoln ride past his regiment in March 1865, and was at Appomattox. He moved to Berkeley at sixty-three, when most men retire, and went on designing for another fifteen years.

He had been a Mason since 1870, so when the Berkeley lodges wanted a temple, they gave him a free hand. He toured temples back East first. Shops took the ground floor. Above them was a banquet hall, and on the third floor a lodge room with a forty-two-foot ceiling.

Rosa Shattuck paid for two pairs of granite pillars at the door, in memory of her husband. A few years later a city ordinance against overhangs took them away, and the entrance was set back.

Wharff died in 1936, in his hundredth year. By then he was thought to be the nation's oldest living Mason and Civil War veteran.
