## guide

The Phi Delta Theta Chapter House, at Hearst Avenue and Highland Place, designed in 1914 by John Reid Junior.

Reid had been a Phi Delta Theta here himself, a sophomore in the old chapter house on Durant Avenue in 1902. He went on to five years at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris, and by 1914 he was one of San Francisco's consulting architects, helping lay out its new Civic Center. He found time to design a new house for his old fraternity: three stories of concrete and frame under a cement plaster skin, built for about 25,000 dollars.

The chapter has its own place in the campus story. One of its early brothers, Jacob Reinstein, of the university's first graduating class, became the first Cal graduate appointed a regent. It was Reinstein who took up Bernard Maybeck's idea of an international competition for the campus plan, brought it to Phoebe Hearst, and championed hiring John Galen Howard.
