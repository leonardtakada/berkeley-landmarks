## guide

You're standing near the West Berkeley Shellmound. There's little to see. The mound itself was leveled long ago, and the ground is paved. But this is one of the oldest and most significant Native American sites around the Bay.

The shoreline once ran close by, at the mouth of Strawberry Creek. The Huichin Ohlone lived here, and over thousands of years the mussel and oyster shells and the earth of their lives built up into a great mound. The oldest layers have been dated to around 3,700 BC. The mound was a village, and later a burial and ceremonial site.

Much of it still lies below the surface, under the parking lots, streets and rail lines around you. The site has long been at the center of efforts to protect it, and of a continuing conversation about honoring Indigenous heritage in a modern city.
