## guide

Greenwood Common. At mid-century the architect William Wurster bought the old Gregory property here and had an idea: instead of a row of separate lots, a cluster of houses sharing one landscaped common. Lawrence Halprin designed the landscape.

Each house was by a different architect, so the common became a kind of exhibition of Bay Area modernism. One of them, by Donald Olsen, became the prototype for his glass house on San Diego Road.

The houses face a shared lawn, and from the common the view runs all the way to the Golden Gate. The common is private, so enjoy it from the lane. It was a new way to live in the hills, and it grew out of the old one: right next door, the Arts and Crafts houses of the turn of the century.

Wurster went on to become dean of the university's College of Environmental Design.
