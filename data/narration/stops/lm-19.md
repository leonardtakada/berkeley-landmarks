## guide

The Studio Building, 1905. Nobody recorded who designed it. It was built by Frederick Dakin, whose company dealt in gold mines and real estate, on foundation bricks from his own works in Stege.

At five stories it was the tallest building downtown until the Shattuck Hotel matched it. Notice what sets it apart from its neighbors: the tile mansard roof and the rounded window bays.

The top floor was the point. It was built as artists' studios with a gallery, and in December 1906 it held its first exhibition, with work by Dakin's brother, the painter Edwin Deakin. One branch of the family spelled the name differently. The architect John Hudson Thomas and the photographer Oscar Maurer had rooms here. After the 1906 earthquake, Frederick Meyer moved his studio over from San Francisco and started the school that became the California College of Arts.

Before you go, look down at the entrance. The tile floor holds an artist's palette and brushes, with the building's name.
