## yelland

William Yelland. Jack Thornburg was twenty-five when he brought me this job, in 1926. He wanted a whole village, shops and all. The zoning wouldn't allow shops, so it became apartments.

Go in through the arch to the round court. The bricks are laid unevenly and pitch toward the middle. The walls mix brick and stone, made to look worn by centuries. Every apartment has its own outside stair, and the floors sit at different levels, so no line is quite straight. Look for the carved heads and gargoyles above the first story.

Inside, the walls looked like hand-hewn stone and timber, and you couldn't find the mark of a milling machine anywhere. When it opened in 1927, the Oakland Tribune called it an "artistic adaptation of the architectural thought of rural Europe."

## guide

A year later Thornburg added Norman Towers, which he designed himself, and that is where the name Normandy Village comes from. He later moved to Phoenix and opened a flying school. One of his students was a young Barry Goldwater.
