## howard

John Galen Howard. In 1904 a company of the university's friends decided the neighborhood north of campus needed a fine residential hotel. Phoebe Hearst was among the investors, and Jane Sather, and, I'll admit, so was I. We named it for George Berkeley, Bishop of Cloyne, the philosopher the town is named for.

I gave it thirty-two suites, from one room to five. There are no long corridors upstairs; every two suites share their own stair. And I took fire seriously. Brick firewalls divide the building, with fireproof doors that slide shut on their own.

It cost eighty thousand dollars, a great deal of money then. Fourteen balconies looked over the courtyard garden and out to the Bay.

## guide

Cloyne Court later became a student cooperative. Newer campus buildings to the west now block most of the view.
