## guide

Piedmont Avenue. Look at the street itself: the planted median down the middle, the gentle curve, the rounded corners. This is the work of Frederick Law Olmsted, the designer of Central Park.

In 1864, while he was in California, the trustees of the College of California asked him to plan their new campus and a neighborhood beside it. His campus plan was never built, but the neighborhood was laid out in 1865, with Piedmont Way at its heart. Olmsted wanted large houses on ample lots, set back behind gardens, along a road sheltered by what he called "an overarching bowery of foliage."

It was his first residential subdivision, and the first of his curving parkways with a divided roadway and a landscaped median. Its ideas went on to Brooklyn's Eastern Parkway, to Buffalo and to Boston.

The lots were slow to sell. The college was far from town and from any transit. But by the early twentieth century the avenue was lined with fine houses in lush gardens, much as Olmsted imagined.
