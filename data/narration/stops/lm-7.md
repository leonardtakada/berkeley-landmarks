## guide

Berkeley City Hall. The town's first hall was a wooden building, and it burned in 1904. In 1907 the trustees held a competition for a new one. Eleven designs came in. John Galen Howard helped them choose, and they picked two young San Francisco architects, John Bakewell and Arthur Brown, both Cal graduates trained at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris.

Their model was the new town hall at Tours, on the Loire, by Brown's teacher in Paris, Victor Laloux. The trustees cut the tower to save money. The citizens wanted it back. It went on and off the plans twice before the city found another 2,400 dollars to build it.

Look just below the tower for the round medallion. A clock was meant to go there. The money ran out, and it has stood empty ever since.

The hall was set a few feet north of the old site so it would line up with the block to the east, which became Civic Center Park thirty-three years later. Bakewell and Brown went on to design San Francisco's City Hall.

City government moved out in 1977. Today the building is named for Maudelle Shirek.
