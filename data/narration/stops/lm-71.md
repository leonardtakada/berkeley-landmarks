## guide

Weltevreden. It's a Dutch word meaning "well satisfied," and it's what Volney and Mary Moody named their retirement house in 1896. Albert Schweinfurth designed it.

He built it of clinker brick, the dark, vitrified brick that kilns used to throw away as refuse. Schweinfurth made it fashionable. He chose the bricks himself, and sometimes laid them himself, to show the masons what he meant. There were Dutch stepped gables at each end, and the entrance was reached by a bridge over the creek. Inside, the living room had gold leaf between the beams, and the dining room was turquoise.

In 1906 a magazine called it the premier residence of Berkeley. And the Moodys' daughters, with a few of their neighbors, founded the Hillside Club, to persuade the whole neighborhood to build in the spirit of Maybeck's houses.

It survived the 1923 fire, became a fraternity house in the 1930s, and was heavily altered in the 1950s. Today it's Tellefsen Hall, home of the Cal Band.
