## guide

The Kawneer factory, built in 1913. Its founder, Francis Plym, was a cabinetmaker's son who became an architect and an inventor. In 1906 he patented a slim, resilient metal frame for shop windows. Wooden frames swelled, shrank and rotted, and cracked large panes of glass. Plym's frames made the big plate-glass shop window possible.

He named it after the Kaw, the river past the Kansas City sheet-metal shop where the first frames were made. After the 1906 earthquake, San Francisco rebuilt with so many of them that a friend suggested renaming Market Street Kawneer Street.

When Plym built this West Coast plant, with Oakland architect Chester Miller, he designed his own product into it. Look up at the roof: twenty sawtooth banks of clerestory windows run the length of the brick building, an early cousin of today's glass walls. For half a century this was the company's West Coast headquarters.
