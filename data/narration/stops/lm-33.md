## guide

The First Unitarian Church, by Albert Schweinfurth, 1898. The congregation was young and short of money, and it counted some remarkable members: Bernard and Annie Maybeck, Charles Keeler, the photographer Oscar Maurer. A grand design with a hundred-foot tower had been drawn and abandoned. Schweinfurth gave them something they could afford, for a little over five thousand dollars.

Look at the west front: one enormous gable, carried on two redwood trunks with the bark still on. The walls are shingled, and the buttresses along the sides are curved, though a wooden building has no need of buttresses. It's a joke about stone churches.

Not everyone got it. A passerby said it looked like a powerhouse. A member answered that it was one.

Schweinfurth died two years later, at thirty-seven, and most of his buildings are gone. In 1959 the university took this property, and the congregation moved to Kensington, onto land bought from the Maybeck family. The church became a dance studio.
