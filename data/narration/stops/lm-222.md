## maybeck

Bernard Maybeck. I first noticed Charles Keeler on the five o'clock ferry from San Francisco. He was about twenty; I was nine or ten years older. I heard he owned a lot up here, with an old live oak on it, near the rim of a little canyon looking out over the Bay. I told him that when he was ready to build, I'd design his house, and I wouldn't charge him for it.

He said he had no intention of building. A few years later he married, and in 1895 he came looking for me. Apart from my own, it was the first house I designed.

The timbers show inside, with redwood planks nailed over them outside. The living room was like a little chapel, open to the peak of the roof, and the plan is a cross that rises with the hill.

Keeler worried that someone would put up white-painted boxes all around him. I told him it was up to him to see that his neighbors built in keeping. Before long, the block had several more of my houses.

## guide

In the 1960s every Maybeck house on this block was torn down for apartments, except two: Keeler's house and his studio next door.
