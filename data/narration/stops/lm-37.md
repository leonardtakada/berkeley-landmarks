## howard

John Galen Howard. I wanted this tower to "rise with a slender stem, bursting into bloom at the summit." No American tower would do, so I went to Venice, to the campanile of San Marco.

In July 1902, the old Venetian tower collapsed into the piazza in a heap of rubble. My first sketches for Berkeley's tower date from the next February. Make of that what you will. Ours is about the same height, 307 feet, but it isn't brick. It's a steel frame clad in granite, and Professor Charles Derleth, our dean of engineering, helped me make sure it would stand.

There are differences, if you know the original. The Venetian belfry has four arches to a side; ours has three. Our shaft is nearly plain, with a single line of slits down the middle. And only ours has the corner obelisks, topped with bronze flames for enlightenment.

## guide

The tower was finished in 1915. Its carillon now has sixty-one bells.
