## maybeck

Bernard Maybeck. Florence Boynton grew up in Oakland with Isadora Duncan, and she believed in living out of doors: sleeping on the roof, dancing on the lawn, dressing like the ancient Greeks. In 1911 she and her husband Charles bought this lot from my wife and me, and asked for a house to match.

I drew them a temple: a ring of giant Corinthian columns on a concrete platform, warmed from below, with canvas curtains instead of walls. While it was being designed, I put up a camp for them here, two pergolas with canvas roofs.

We fell out over the budget and a right of way, and I withdrew. Randolph Monro, a young draftsman from the Hillside Club, finished it in 1914.

## guide

The 1923 fire left only the concrete columns. In 1924 the cousins Edna Deakin and Clarence Dakin built a two-story house within them, with a dance studio in each wing. Generations of Berkeley children learned to dance here, and the Boyntons' summer dance festival went on until 1985.
