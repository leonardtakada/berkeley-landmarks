## guide

Westminster Presbyterian Church, 1879, the second-oldest church standing in Berkeley, a year younger than its neighbor, the Church of the Good Shepherd. Charles Geddes of San Francisco designed it, in wood, in the Gothic Revival style. The same year he drew the chapel that still stands in Yosemite Valley.

The congregation was small and always poor. It seldom reached seventy-five members, and it sometimes blamed the saloons. West Berkeley lay outside the dry zone around the university, so liquor was easy to come by. One pastor, during a fight over local option, was hung in effigy in the middle of the street.

In 1899 they began calling themselves Westminster, to avoid confusion with another Presbyterian church in town. They never made the change legal, because that would have cost a dollar at the probate court. Even so, after the 1906 earthquake they took in refugees from San Francisco.
