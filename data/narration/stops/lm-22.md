## guide

The Shattuck Hotel stands on the homestead of Francis Kittredge Shattuck, the man who built Berkeley's first downtown. He always meant to put a grand hotel here, and he died before he could.

In 1907 a group of Berkeley businessmen took up the idea. Five architects competed, and Benjamin McDougall, known for public buildings in the San Joaquin Valley, won. They gave him six months, and money to travel for ideas. He came back with arched windows and square towers at the corners.

Work began in July 1909. The hotel was promised by Christmas and opened a year late, in December 1910, with a week of parties. At the Chamber of Commerce banquet the last of twelve courses was crackers: Grant's Hygienic Crackers, made in Berkeley. The poet Joaquin Miller spoke, and predicted that the cities around the bay would one day be one.

In 1913 a 120-room wing went up in time for visitors to the 1915 Exposition, and Hink's department store moved into its ground floor for the next seven decades.
