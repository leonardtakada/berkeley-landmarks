## guide

The Claremont Hotel. It was part of a bigger scheme. Early in the last century, the Key Route's electric trains and ferries were linking the East Bay to San Francisco, and a grand hotel on fourteen acres of garden would draw people out to the new neighborhoods along the lines. It was built for Francis "Borax" Smith, founder of the Pacific Coast Borax Company, and the Berkeley attorney Frank Havens.

Charles Dickey of Oakland won the design competition, and ground was broken in 1906. It was meant to be seen from all around the Bay. It opened in 1915, and it has been called "California's Hotel Beautiful."

Around it, Duncan McDuffie laid out the Claremont district, with roads following the curve of the land. In 1991 the Oakland Hills firestorm came down the ridge, and the hotel and the houses near it were saved.
