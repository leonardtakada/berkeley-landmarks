## guide

The H. J. Heinz factory. From San Pablo Avenue it could pass for a high school or a college: a long, symmetrical front of beige stucco under a red tile roof, with an arched entrance and an open loggia above it. Behind that front was a working factory, built between 1927 and 1928 and running two blocks along what was then the main highway.

The architect was Albert Kahn, who designed a large share of America's industrial buildings, and this is believed to be his only one in Berkeley. When it opened, the local paper predicted it would be one of the most beautiful industrial buildings in the West.

By 1947 it was producing twenty-eight of Heinz's "57 Varieties," with up to a thousand workers in season. During tomato season, the smell of cooking tomatoes filled the air. The plant closed in 1956, and the building has been remodeled into shops and offices, with an art institute among its tenants.
