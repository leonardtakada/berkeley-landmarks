## ratcliff

I'm Walter Ratcliff. I was Berkeley's first city architect, and I spent most of my working life in this town.

In 1925 the Central Berkeley Building Company asked me for something Berkeley had never had: a tall building. Twelve stories. People called it a skyscraper, and for forty-five years it was the only one we had.

The Chamber of Commerce took the top floor, and they knew exactly why. Charles Keeler ran the chamber then, and he could stand a visitor at the window and show him the bay, the hills, and the Golden Gate straight across the water.

The bank downstairs, American Trust, kept the Stanford Axe in its vault. In April of 1930 a band of Stanford students, some of them posing as photographers, snatched it on the steps out front and used a tear gas bomb to make their escape.

## guide

The building later took the Wells Fargo name. An easement held by the California Preservation Foundation now protects its face on Shattuck Avenue.
