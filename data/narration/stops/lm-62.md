## guide

South Hall, 1873, the oldest building on the campus and the only one left from the university's beginning. David Farquharson designed it, in brick and stone, in the Second Empire style of Napoleon the Third's Paris. Notice the mansard roof.

It was one of two original buildings. Its partner, North Hall, was built of wood and is long gone. South Hall did everything at first: it held the library, the museums and the laboratories, and by 1879 a physics laboratory. Later it housed agriculture, a business school and a museum for the state geological survey.

Its front door used to face west. When Wheeler Hall was planned, the entrance was moved to the east side.

Before you go, look at the balcony railing above the entrance for a small stone bear, by the sculptor Michael Casey. It's said to be the smallest bear on campus.
