## guide

The Golden Sheaf Bakery. In 1877 an English baker, Jonathan Wright, opened Berkeley's first wholesale and retail bakery around the corner on Shattuck Avenue. It grew fast: twenty-six kinds of bread, a dozen kinds of cakes and pies, a dining room, catering, and by 1905 a fleet of forty wagons and motor cars.

This brick building went up in 1905 as the bakery's storehouse and loading dock. Even so, the owners hired a serious architect, Clinton Day. Look up at the center: a sheaf of wheat molded in terra cotta, with the bakery's name beneath it. Four pilasters divide the front into three bays, with paired arched windows above.

The next spring, after the 1906 earthquake and fire, the bakery turned out bread for the thousands left homeless across the bay.

The building became a garage in 1927 and now serves the theaters of Addison Street. It is one of the few early brick buildings left downtown.
